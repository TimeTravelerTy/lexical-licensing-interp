# Gemma replication: controls for the early-layer DAS gap, and transfer of the active-trained direction

Tables:
- `das_controls.md` (A; `scripts/analyze_das_controls.py`);
- `transfer.md` (B; `scripts/analyze_gemma_transfer.py`);
- the per-analysis Gemma reports in `transfer/` (generated by the unchanged Pythia analysis scripts).

Logs: `results/gemma/logs/` (`g_ctl_*`, `g_pythia_psweep_fill`, `g_final_gemma_*`, `g_tr_*`).

## A. Controls (both models, every site, active and passive tasks)

Setup:
- Held-out sets are the sweep's: split 0, 5 folds, the same held-out verbs as the token split. The runner is
  `scripts/run_das_controls.py`.
- **Random rank-1 baseline:** 20 untrained unit directions per site, drawn as the final runs draw theirs
  (QR of a Gaussian). They go through the unchanged interchange.
- **Probe:** logistic regression (sklearn, L2, C = 1, standardized) for T vs I, on the verb-position states.
  It is fit on each fold's DAS training items and scored on its held-out items.
- **Trained IIA:** the sweep's best-epoch fold mean.
- **Pythia passive trained IIA:** the stored reverse sweep covers sites 4–17 only. The other sites come from a
  fill-in sweep run today with the same arguments (`results/gemma/das/pythia_passive_sweep_fill`; the stored
  sweep is untouched).

Findings:
- **Random directions do nothing.** At every site in both models, the random baseline equals the unpatched
  floor:
  - mean: active 0.019–0.020 (Pythia), 0.005–0.008 (Gemma); passive 0.191–0.192 (Pythia), 0.204–0.213 (Gemma);
  - the 95th percentile is within 0.015 of the unpatched floor at every site but the last.

  So both models' trained IIA is far above this floor at every site, early sites included, and the early-layer
  gap is not something a random rank-1 interchange reproduces. The floor is not 0.5 chance: early trained IIA
  is above the floor but below 0.5 at Pythia's active sites 0–5 (0.07–0.55) and at Gemma's active site 0
  (0.36). The final runs' norm-matched random controls agree:
  mean ≤ 0.02 at every transfer site in both models.
- **Full-dimensional linear decodability is near ceiling in both models from site 3, so it does not separate
  them there.**
  - Active task. Pythia's probe is 0.97–1.00 from site 3, with trained IIA 0.29 (site 3) to 0.67 (site 6).
    Over the same depth, Gemma's probe is 0.96–1.00 and its trained IIA 0.76–0.89. Only at sites 0–2 is
    Pythia's probe lower (0.85–0.90 vs 0.96).
  - Passive task. Pythia's probe is 0.91–0.98 from site 1 (0.78 at site 0); Gemma's is 0.95–1.00.
  - At sites 3–8 a full-rank probe reads the class equally well in both models; what differs is what a
    rank-1 interchange achieves. That can be causal efficacy, but equal probe accuracy does not establish equal
    geometry: how much of the class lies along one direction, or how easily DAS finds it. At sites 0–2,
    Pythia's lower probe accuracy is a decodability difference too.
- **The shuffled-label control is higher in Gemma.** This comes from the final runs (`final_strict*/summary.csv`),
  so only at the transfer sites. DAS trained on permuted verb classes reaches a mean held-out IIA of
  0.41 / 0.41 in Gemma (sites 12 / 15; fold max 0.98 / 0.99), against 0.20 / 0.24 / 0.34 in Pythia (sites 8 / 12 / 17).
  - A permutation that happens to correlate with the true classes, in either sign, still lets a rank-1
    interchange find the class axis.
  - Higher shuffled IIA therefore fits a class axis in Gemma that is easier to land on and more causally
    effective. It is also a warning that Gemma's IIA has a higher control floor than Pythia's. It does not
    make the trained IIA (0.94 / 0.98) spurious.
- **Caveats:**
  - The probe's held-out unit is a verb pair (about 6 per fold). Accuracies are item-level: 7 subjects per verb (active) or 15
    contexts (passive: 12 training, 3 held-out, all scored for held-out verbs). So they move in coarse steps.
  - Pythia's stored trained IIA is from October. Today's environment differs by ≤ 0.01 per fold (token split).
  - The last site in both models uses post-final-norm source states and is reported only.

## B. Transfer on Gemma-3-1B with the active-trained direction

**Sites, by the declared rules only:**
- **Site 15**, the frozen rule of `select_das_config.py` on the Gemma active sweep: epochs 2, rank 1 chosen by
  the final run's rank rule (pc_frac 1.18 / 1.19 / 1.23 for ranks 1 / 2 / 4).
- **Site 12**, the earliest site whose best active IIA is within 0.05 of the max (0.9996, site 20): 0.9554
  against a threshold of 0.9496. Site 11 (0.9453) just misses. Epochs 3, from the frozen epoch rule.
- Relative depths are 0.46 and 0.58. Pythia's sites with the "abstract" passive reading are 4–8, at depth
  0.17–0.33. No Gemma site at that depth was tested, so that reading is not tested on Gemma here.

**Pipeline, exactly as for Pythia:**
- Final runs: `run_das_round2.py final`, 3 splits × 5 folds, with the round-2 final arguments.
- Bases: `export_bases.py`.
- TOST bounds: `analyze_passive_test.py delta`, δ_s = 0.2 × the site's held-out active ΔlogP(O). Gemma:
  0.58 (site 15), 0.56 (site 12); Pythia 0.42–0.57. The same code reproduces Pythia's stored site-17 and
  site-8 bounds exactly.
- Builders: rerun with Gemma's own final-run items and folds. Gemma's behaviour filter drops transplant/ensue,
  so those verbs are non-DAS for Gemma. Fed Pythia's items and folds, the builders reproduce Pythia's committed
  passive-test, construction and filler-gap inputs byte for byte, plans included.
- Runners and analyses are unchanged apart from site options (defaults = Pythia's sites).
  - Passive, OR / TC and in-range run in bf16, as Pythia.
  - Filler-gap follows the round-5 C8 protocol: fp32, non-DAS primary population, both target pools. Gemma's
    kept I verbs come from the round-4 half-A I-verb gate applied to its own fp32 natural pass. Pythia's came
    from its round-4 bf16 pass, and an fp32 gate would have kept the same verbs.

**Fidelity and gates:**
- Self-patch and self-set reproduce the unpatched readouts: max |dM| 0 in bf16, ≤ 2.1e-5 in fp32.
- Recomputing 512 held-out active swaps of each final run gives median |dM| 0.000 and max ≤ 0.07.
- Every natural gate passes:
  - OR: L +1.80 [+1.48, +2.14];
  - TC: L +0.67 [+0.45, +0.92]; TC base-form AUC 0.99;
  - embedded wh: interaction L +1.10 [+0.71, +1.46], O +0.71 [+0.30, +1.11];
  - matrix wh: L +1.05 [+0.64, +1.47], O +1.75 [+1.41, +2.07]; matrix base-form AUC 1.00.
- Gemma keeps fewer I verbs: 16 / 26 non-DAS (embedded / matrix), against Pythia's 28 / 34.

**Results, Gemma next to Pythia at matched depth** (`transfer.md` has the numbers):
- **Passive (*by*).** Both Gemma sites read **mixed** (O RISE and *by* RISE), like Pythia's sites 10–12 (depth
  0.42–0.50).
  - O rises more than at Pythia's matched site: +1.96 at Gemma 12 vs +1.17 at Pythia 12; +2.94 at Gemma 15 vs
    +2.40 at Pythia 14.
  - *By* still rises at depth 0.58 in Gemma (+0.86 [+0.76, +0.96]). At Pythia 14 it is NO RISE (+0.46).
  - Gemma's natural *by* gap is smaller, so D(*by*) is 2.1–2.3 × that gap (Pythia 0.6–1.1).
- **Object relatives, tough constructions (T − I donors).** D(O) and D(L) are RISE at both Gemma sites, as at
  every Pythia site from 8 up (Pythia TC D(O) is NO RISE at site 4 and unresolved at 6). At depth 0.58 Gemma's
  D(L) is larger than Pythia's (OR +2.09 vs +0.96; TC +1.31 vs +0.59), and its D(O) slightly smaller.
  - Gemma's TC D(L) comes mostly from PREP falling (−0.76 / −1.04): END rises only +0.08 [−0.01, +0.18] at
    site 12 and +0.27 [+0.15, +0.41] at site 15.
- **In range (coordinate set to the construction's own class means).** At depth 0.58, Gemma keeps more of the
  MAIN / END numerator effect than Pythia. Ratios to the natural gap, Gemma 15 vs Pythia 14:
  - OR MAIN 0.48 vs 0.10;
  - TC END 0.59 vs 0.00;
  - passive *by* 0.46 vs 0.29.

  At depth 0.46–0.50 the two models are similar: OR MAIN 0.44 vs 0.36, TC END 0.46 vs 0.46. In both models the
  in-range passive patch also raises O (Gemma +0.40 / +0.43, Pythia 12 / 14 +0.26 / +0.34), although the natural
  T − I O gap on passives is negative (Gemma −0.19).
- **Filler-gap.**
  - T − I donors read **surface (object next)** at both Gemma sites, in both frames, as at every Pythia site
    from 6 up.
  - Embedded wh in range: **mixed / unresolved** at both Gemma sites, against surface at Pythia 12–17.
  - Matrix wh in range: **surface** at both Gemma sites. Pythia is surface at 12–17 and licensing-like only at
    4, 8 and 10.
  - In range, Gemma's filler lowers the patched O (fill − nofill O −0.76 / −1.19 for matrix wh; −0.17 /
    −0.31 for embedded). But the O level stays above the natural T level (+0.95 / +0.73), so the declared
    reading is surface.

**Reading.** At the two rule-picked sites, the Gemma direction has causal effects in all five constructions,
and every gate passes.
- The effects are largest on O and PREP: objects rise and prepositions fall. The strongest alternative to a
  construction-specific reading is therefore general continuation steering ("an object comes next").
  - Gemma's TC D(L) is mostly PREP.
  - The in-range passive patch raises O against the natural gap.
  - The matrix and embedded filler-gap patches read surface or mixed, not licensing-like.
- Against Pythia at the same relative depth:
  - Depth 0.46: the profiles agree. Passive is mixed in both, OR / TC rise, filler-gap T − I donors read
    surface.
  - Depth 0.58: they differ. Gemma's passive is still mixed (*by* RISE) where Pythia's is surface. Gemma's
    in-range MAIN / END numerators persist where Pythia's have faded. Gemma's embedded-wh in range is
    mixed / unresolved where Pythia's is surface.
  - So Gemma at 0.58 looks more like Pythia at about 0.42–0.50.
- Whether Gemma has Pythia's early "abstract" profile (O NO RISE, *by* RISE) is not tested: the rules picked
  no early site. No nonce runs.

# Gemma replication: controls for the early-layer DAS gap, and transfer of the active-trained direction

Tables:
- `das_controls.md` (A; `scripts/analyze_das_controls.py`);
- `transfer.md` (B; `scripts/analyze_gemma_transfer.py`);
- the per-analysis Gemma reports in `transfer/` (generated by the unchanged Pythia analysis scripts).

Logs: `results/gemma/logs/` (`g_ctl_*`, `g_pythia_psweep_fill`, `g_final_gemma_*`, `g_tr_*`). The transfer chain
was rerun at all four Gemma sites (jobs 9281244–9281248); sites 12 and 15 reproduce the first run exactly.

## A. Controls (both models, every site, active and passive tasks)

Setup:
- Held-out sets are the sweep's: split 0, 5 folds, the same held-out verbs as the token split. The runner is
  `scripts/run_das_controls.py`.
- **Random rank-1 baseline:** 20 untrained unit directions per site, drawn as the final runs draw theirs
  (QR of a Gaussian). They go through the unchanged interchange.
- **Probe:** logistic regression (sklearn, L2, C = 1, standardized) for T vs I, on the verb-position states.
  It is fit on each fold's DAS training items and scored on its held-out items.
- **Trained IIA:** the sweep's best-epoch fold mean.
- **Pythia passive trained IIA:** the stored reverse sweep covers sites 4–17 only. The other sites come from a
  fill-in sweep run today with the same arguments (`results/gemma/das/pythia_passive_sweep_fill`; the stored
  sweep is untouched).

Findings:
- **Random directions do nothing.** At every site in both models, the random baseline equals the unpatched
  floor:
  - mean: active 0.019–0.020 (Pythia), 0.005–0.008 (Gemma); passive 0.191–0.192 (Pythia), 0.204–0.213 (Gemma);
  - the 95th percentile is within 0.015 of the unpatched floor at every site but the last.

  So both models' trained IIA is far above this floor at every site, early sites included, and the early-layer
  gap is not something a random rank-1 interchange reproduces. The floor is not 0.5 chance: early trained IIA
  is above the floor but below 0.5 at Pythia's active sites 0–5 (0.07–0.55) and at Gemma's active site 0
  (0.36). The final runs' norm-matched random controls agree:
  mean ≤ 0.02 at every transfer site in both models.
- **Full-dimensional linear decodability is near ceiling in both models from site 3, so it does not separate
  them there.**
  - Active task. Pythia's probe is 0.97–1.00 from site 3, with trained IIA 0.29 (site 3) to 0.67 (site 6).
    Over the same depth, Gemma's probe is 0.96–1.00 and its trained IIA 0.76–0.89. Only at sites 0–2 is
    Pythia's probe lower (0.85–0.90 vs 0.96).
  - Passive task. Pythia's probe is 0.91–0.98 from site 1 (0.78 at site 0); Gemma's is 0.95–1.00.
  - At sites 3–8 a full-rank probe reads the class equally well in both models; what differs is what a
    rank-1 interchange achieves. That can be causal efficacy, but equal probe accuracy does not establish equal
    geometry: how much of the class lies along one direction, or how easily DAS finds it. At sites 0–2,
    Pythia's lower probe accuracy is a decodability difference too.
- **The shuffled-label control is higher in Gemma.** This comes from the final runs (`final_strict*/summary.csv`),
  so only at the transfer sites. DAS trained on permuted verb classes reaches a mean held-out IIA of
  0.29 / 0.35 / 0.41 / 0.41 in Gemma (sites 5 / 8 / 12 / 15; fold max 0.55 / 0.77 / 0.98 / 0.99). Pythia's range is
  0.13–0.34 over sites 4–17 (0.20 at 8, 0.24 at 12, 0.34 at 17).
  - A permutation that happens to correlate with the true classes, in either sign, still lets a rank-1
    interchange find the class axis.
  - Higher shuffled IIA therefore fits a class axis in Gemma that is easier to land on and more causally
    effective. It is also a warning that Gemma's IIA has a higher control floor than Pythia's. It does not
    make the trained IIA (0.85 / 0.88 / 0.94 / 0.98) spurious.
- **Caveats:**
  - The probe's held-out unit is a verb pair (about 6 per fold). Accuracies are item-level: 7 subjects per verb (active) or 15
    contexts (passive: 12 training, 3 held-out, all scored for held-out verbs). So they move in coarse steps.
  - Pythia's stored trained IIA is from October. Today's environment differs by ≤ 0.01 per fold (token split).
  - The last site in both models uses post-final-norm source states and is reported only.

## B. Transfer on Gemma-3-1B with the active-trained direction

**Sites, by declared rules only:**
- **Site 15**, the frozen rule of `select_das_config.py` on the Gemma active sweep: epochs 2, rank 1 chosen by
  the final run's rank rule (pc_frac 1.18 / 1.19 / 1.23 for ranks 1 / 2 / 4).
- **Site 12**, the earliest site whose best active IIA is within 0.05 of the max (0.9996, site 20): 0.9554
  against a threshold of 0.9496. Site 11 (0.9453) just misses. Epochs 3, from the frozen epoch rule.
- **Sites 5 and 8**, added to test Pythia's early "abstract" passive profile, which Pythia shows at its sites 4–8
  (depth 0.17–0.33).
  - The rule is Pythia's own declared secondary-site rule (`passive_test_plan.md`): the first sites whose best
    active IIA reaches about 0.8 and about 0.9. On Pythia's sweep it gives Pythia's sites 8 and 12.
  - On Gemma's sweep it gives site 5 (0.888; site 4 is 0.793, just below 0.8) and site 8 (0.903). Epochs 2 and 3,
    from the frozen epoch rule.
- Relative depths: 0.19, 0.31, 0.46, 0.58.

**Pipeline, exactly as for Pythia:**
- Final runs: `run_das_round2.py final`, 3 splits × 5 folds, with the round-2 final arguments.
- Bases: `export_bases.py`.
- TOST bounds: `analyze_passive_test.py delta`, δ_s = 0.2 × the site's held-out active ΔlogP(O).
  - Gemma: 0.54 / 0.53 / 0.56 / 0.58 at sites 5 / 8 / 12 / 15. Pythia: 0.42–0.57.
  - The same code reproduces Pythia's stored site-17 and site-8 bounds exactly.
- Builders: rerun with Gemma's own final-run items and folds.
  - Gemma's behaviour filter drops transplant/ensue, so those verbs are non-DAS for Gemma.
  - Fed Pythia's items and folds, the builders reproduce Pythia's committed passive-test, construction and
    filler-gap inputs byte for byte, plans included.
- Runners and analyses are unchanged apart from site options (defaults = Pythia's sites).
  - Passive, OR / TC and in-range run in bf16, as Pythia.
  - Filler-gap follows the round-5 C8 protocol: fp32, non-DAS primary population, both target pools.
  - Gemma's kept I verbs come from the round-4 half-A I-verb gate applied to its own fp32 natural pass.
    Pythia's came from its round-4 bf16 pass; an fp32 gate would have kept the same verbs.

**Fidelity and gates:**
- Final-run DAS IIA (mean over the 15 folds): Gemma 0.85 / 0.88 / 0.94 / 0.98 at sites 5 / 8 / 12 / 15. Pythia:
  0.45 / 0.66 / 0.78 / 0.82 / 0.90 / 0.95 at sites 4 / 6 / 8 / 10 / 12 / 14. Norm-matched random directions
  give ≤ 0.01 at every Gemma site.
- Self-patch and self-set reproduce the unpatched readouts: max |dM| 0 in bf16, ≤ 2.3e-5 in fp32.
- Recomputing 512 held-out active swaps of each final run gives median |dM| 0.000 and max ≤ 0.091.
- Every gate passes:
  - the natural gates, which are site-independent:
    - OR: L +1.80 [+1.48, +2.14];
    - TC: L +0.67 [+0.45, +0.92];
    - embedded wh: interaction L +1.10 [+0.71, +1.46], O +0.71 [+0.30, +1.11];
    - matrix wh: L +1.05 [+0.64, +1.47], O +1.75 [+1.41, +2.07];
  - the base-form AUCs along the direction, per site 5 / 8 / 12 / 15: TC 0.97 / 0.98 / 0.99 / 0.99; matrix
    1.00 at every site.
- Gemma keeps fewer I verbs: 16 / 26 non-DAS (embedded / matrix), against Pythia's 28 / 34.

**Results, Gemma next to Pythia at matched depth** (`transfer.md` has the numbers):
- **Passive (*by*). Every Gemma site reads mixed (O RISE and *by* RISE). No Gemma site reads abstract.**
  - Early sites: Gemma 5 / 8 (depth 0.19 / 0.31):
    - O +1.50 [+1.29, +1.72] / +1.38 [+1.24, +1.53], far above δ (0.54 / 0.53);
    - *by* +0.56 / +0.63, RISE.
  - Pythia 4 / 6 / 8 (depth 0.17–0.33) read abstract: O +0.10 / +0.23 / +0.36 is NO RISE, and *by* +0.58 / +0.69
    / +0.72 is RISE.
  - So the *by* effect is about the same size in the two models at this depth. The difference is all in O.
  - O as a fraction of the site's active effect (δ_s / 0.2):
    - Gemma 0.56 / 0.52 / 0.70 / 1.02 at sites 5 / 8 / 12 / 15;
    - Pythia 0.05 / 0.10 / 0.15 at sites 4 / 6 / 8, then 0.28 / 0.44 / 0.87 at sites 10 / 12 / 14.

    Gemma's early direction moves the passive object continuation half as much as it moves the active one;
    Pythia's barely moves it.
  - Later sites: O rises more than at Pythia's matched site: +1.96 at Gemma 12 vs +1.17 at Pythia 12; +2.94 at
    Gemma 15 vs +2.40 at Pythia 14. *By* still rises at depth 0.58 in Gemma (+0.86 [+0.76, +0.96]); at Pythia 14
    it is NO RISE (+0.46).
  - Gemma's natural *by* gap is smaller, so D(*by*) is 1.4–2.3 × that gap (Pythia 0.6–1.1).
- **Object relatives, tough constructions (T − I donors).** D(O) and D(L) are RISE at every Gemma site.
  - TC D(O) is RISE at Gemma 5 / 8 (+1.14 / +1.30). Pythia's matched TC D(O) is NO RISE at site 4 (+0.30) and
    unresolved at 6 (+0.37). This is the same O-first difference as on passives.
  - Early on, Gemma's licensed-continuation rise is smaller than Pythia's:
    - OR MAIN: +0.56 / +0.87 at Gemma 5 / 8, against +1.34 to +1.49 at Pythia 4–8;
    - TC END: +0.24 / +0.15, against +0.72 to +0.80.

    PREP falls in both (Gemma −0.41 to −0.61, Pythia −0.57 to −0.80).
  - At depth 0.58, Gemma's D(L) is larger than Pythia's (OR +2.09 vs +0.96; TC +1.31 vs +0.59) and its D(O)
    slightly smaller. Gemma's TC D(L) there comes mostly from PREP falling: END rises only +0.08 [−0.01, +0.18]
    at site 12 and +0.27 [+0.15, +0.41] at site 15.
- **In range (coordinate set to the construction's own class means).** Ratios to the natural gap:
  - Early (Gemma 5 / 8 vs Pythia 4–8):
    - OR MAIN 0.65 / 0.68 vs 0.79–0.93;
    - TC END 0.93 / 0.70 vs 0.91–1.10;
    - passive *by* 0.76 / 0.89 vs 0.44–0.52.

    The in-range patch also raises O on passives in Gemma, although the natural T − I O gap there is negative:
    +0.27 / +0.50, against +0.04 to +0.09 in Pythia. On TC the O ratio is 1.67 / 2.22, against 0.44–1.01.
  - At depth 0.58, Gemma keeps more of the MAIN / END numerator effect than Pythia (Gemma 15 vs Pythia 14):
    OR MAIN 0.48 vs 0.10, TC END 0.59 vs 0.00, passive *by* 0.46 vs 0.29.
  - At depth 0.46–0.50 the two models are similar: OR MAIN 0.44 vs 0.36, TC END 0.46 vs 0.46.
- **Filler-gap.**
  - T − I donors read **surface (object next)** at every Gemma site, in both frames. Pythia reads surface at
    every site except matrix wh at site 4 (mixed / unresolved).
  - Embedded wh in range: Gemma 5 surface, 8 / 12 / 15 mixed / unresolved. Pythia 4, 8 and 10 are
    mixed / unresolved; 6 and 12–17 are surface.
  - Matrix wh in range: **surface at every Gemma site**. Pythia is licensing-like at 4, 8 and 10, mixed at 6,
    and surface at 12–17.
  - At sites 8–15, Gemma's filler lowers the patched O in matrix wh (fill − nofill O −0.67 / −0.76 / −1.19).
    But the O level stays above the natural T level (+0.82 / +0.95 / +0.73), so the declared reading is surface.

**Reading.** At all four rule-picked sites, the Gemma direction has causal effects in all five constructions,
and every gate passes.
- **On the declared test, Gemma does not show Pythia's early abstract profile at the tested sites.** At depth 0.19 and 0.31, the *by* effect matches
  Pythia's. But the direction also raises the passive object continuation well past the equivalence bound, and
  the same holds for TC objects. In matrix wh, the in-range patch never gives Pythia's licensing-like crossover.
- **Depth and IIA are confounded.** Pythia's abstract sites are also its low-IIA sites (final IIA 0.45–0.78).
  Gemma's earliest rule-picked sites already reach 0.85 / 0.88, the level of Pythia's sites 10 / 12 (0.82 /
  0.90), which also read mixed.
  - Matched by IIA rather than depth, the two models agree on the passive outcome. Even so, Gemma's O fraction
    (0.52–0.56) exceeds Pythia's at those sites (0.28 / 0.44).
  - Gemma sites with Pythia's abstract-range IIA exist only at depth ≤ 0.15 (sweep IIA 0.64–0.79 at sites 1–4).
    No declared rule picks them. They were run afterwards as exploratory IIA-matched sites 0 / 1 / 4 (`exploratory.md`), which does not change this declared reading.
  - So the data do not say whether Gemma has an abstract stage at all, or has one only before its class
    direction becomes high-IIA.
- **The O rise depends on the donor values.** The declared passive outcome is defined on active-donor patches,
  which carry active-context coordinate values into passive bases.
  - With the coordinate set to the passives' own class means instead (in range), Gemma's early passive O
    effect drops from +1.50 / +1.38 to +0.27 / +0.50, a reduction of 82% / 64%. *By* keeps 76% / 89% of its
    natural gap.
  - So much of the early O rise may be the active donors' scale overshooting on passive bases, rather than a
    surface profile.
  - Even in range, Gemma's passive O stays above Pythia's (+0.04 to +0.09 at sites 4–8). The in-range analysis
    has no declared equivalence test, so this does not turn the declared reading into abstract.
  - "Gemma does not show the abstract profile" holds for the declared test, at the tested sites, with
    active donors.
- **The other alternative is general continuation steering ("an object comes next").** In Gemma it
  fits the early sites better than in Pythia:
  - O rises on passives and TC at every site;
  - PREP falls everywhere;
  - MAIN / END rise less than in Pythia early on;
  - the filler-gap patches read surface or mixed, never licensing-like.
- **Later sites:**
  - Depth 0.46: the profiles agree. Passive is mixed in both, OR / TC rise, filler-gap T − I donors read
    surface.
  - Depth 0.58: they differ. Gemma's passive is still mixed (*by* RISE) where Pythia's is surface. Gemma's
    in-range MAIN / END numerators persist where Pythia's have faded. Gemma's embedded-wh in range is
    mixed / unresolved where Pythia's is surface.
  - So Gemma at 0.58 looks more like Pythia at about 0.42–0.50.
- No nonce runs.

## Contribution and question

**One sentence.** We identify a verb-site coordinate associated with transitivity that supports causal transfer across held-out verbs and constructions in three language models, measure how much of a verb's passive *by* preference depends on it, and dissect the voice-sensitive components that read it in Pythia.

**Organizing question (reworded Nov 26 to what the paper answers).** Do language models encode a verb's transitivity in a form shared across verbs and constructions, and if so, how much of their behavior depends on it and how is it read? (No premise: sharing across constructions is shown by the construction-appropriate predictions of one edit; across verbs by held-out transfer and cross-fitted LEACE.) The "what information" part is answered descriptively: a graded position along the direction, whose within-class order is tested against object bias and passive share (C1, verb-position figure, graded check), read by neurons that detect a transitive verb whose object is missing (C4). Do not claim more than that about its content. Lookup vs computation is not the frame (the thesis has the more nuanced version); the discussion says in a paragraph how the results bear on it. The paper does not claim a verdict: the in-sample vs cross-fitted LEACE difference is not an additive "per-verb" component (fit size, covariance and the scrub sequence all differ; Settled 13), and with more training verbs the cross-fitted share keeps rising (0.59 → 0.42 remaining on the same items, Settled 16).

## Thesis, with the limits next to it

- A verb-site coordinate associated with transitivity, sensitive to surface NP cues (nonce, R4-C9), supports interventions that transfer across several constructions. On the held-out evaluation set (224 pairs; 89 plain primary) the v2 edit moves passive *by* by about the natural gap at the first rule site of Pythia and OLMo (D / G 1.05, 0.89; a shuffled-label DAS comparator 0.71, 0.66), and by more than the gap in Gemma (1.8–2.5× from site 8). The object side is model- and construction-dependent: the edit matches the natural object-relative profile only at the first OR site of Pythia and OLMo, and overshoots the natural object effect elsewhere.
- Erasers fit on other verbs reduce the passive *by* gap by 0.53 [0.44, 0.67] (Pythia), 0.83 [0.61, 1.22] (Gemma) and 0.57 [0.45, 0.74] (OLMo). These are pooled reductions of a T − I gap, not per-verb fractions. Say "about half or more", never "at least half" (the Pythia and OLMo lower bounds are below 0.5).
- In Pythia, a passive-conjunction set in MLPs 11 / 14, selected with v2 on half the held-out pairs, carries 0.62 [0.58, 0.66] of those layers' edit-induced *by* switch on the other half. Clamping it mediates 0.19 [0.15, 0.23] of the edit's T − I effect on *by* (activation-matched neurons 0.01; all of MLPs 11 + 14 0.26). The training-free u_md gives the same numbers on the frozen set. Most of the edit's effect reaches the output through the residual stream itself (0.58 with every component at the participle clamped). Swapping only the v2 coordinate between natural paired sentences removes 0.14 of the model's own gap, and P mediates 0.27 of that; P's neuron swap alone does not detectably carry the natural gap.

Words to avoid: "one feature" (rank-1 success is not uniqueness; class information survives erasure), "DAS identifies the verb's class" or "the labels pick out the direction" (a shuffled-label DAS lands at |cos| 0.70–0.76, see C1), "label-free" for u_md (it is a class-mean difference: "fit without DAS optimization"), "every construction", "an NP follows this verb" as the meaning for known verbs, "circuit".

- **Main, controls in Figure 1 (Nov 26; `reports/v2_controls/c1_controls_<m>.md`, rules Part A):** v2 and a shuffled-label DAS comparator side by side in D / G. First rule site, *by*, eval plain: Pythia 1.05 vs 0.71, Gemma 1.27 vs 0.80 (site 5), OLMo 0.89 vs 0.66. Random directions 0.00 (within ±0.1 G, A2). Declared A1 (v2 exceeds the comparator) holds.
  - **Reading (after Codex, Nov 26):** the comparator is not a null. Its |cos| with v2 is 0.70–0.76 (Gemma 5: 0.56, one fold 0.12; Gemma 8: 0.73), close to what the objective gives when labels carry no information: if the margin is read along one axis and a direction sits at cosine c to it, a swap mixes the margins as (1 − c²) base + c² source, and with uninformative targets BCE favors averaging, c = 1/√2. So the overlap says the objective finds the axis that moves the object-vs-intransitive margin; it says nothing about what the labels add, and v2 − comparator is not "what the labels contribute".
  - **What carries the class link instead:** D is scored with the true classes of held-out donors (held-out T and I verbs separate along the direction and the swap carries the difference into passives); u_md (class means, no DAS optimization) transfers too; C3. The text says "a verb-site coordinate associated with transitivity", never that DAS identifies the class.
  - **Consequence for cosines:** any |cos| between DAS directions (v vs v2, the disjoint-sample curve) has a floor near 0.7 from the objective, not ≈ 0.02 from random directions. Report such cosines as observed overlaps beside the comparator's, not as similarity evidence.
  - Not run (decided): a refit with 0.5 targets on class-agnostic swaps, which would show the pseudo-labels are dispensable. The algebra already explains the overlap.

### C4. How Pythia reads it (§5; v2 on the held-out set, `reports/pythia/v2_mech/readings.md`)

**Story (Nov 27, after phase 1b; `reports/v2_controls_mech_b_readings.md`):** three parts, in this order. (1) The edit's path: most of its *by* effect reaches the output through the residual stream itself; MLPs 11 / 14 / 15 amplify it, MLPs 12 / 23 oppose it, and P mediates 0.19, five neurons 0.17. (2) The link to the model's own behavior is the coordinate, not the neuron swap: the natural v2 coordinate carries part of the gap, and P mediates a quarter of that piece. (3) The neurons that read it have distinct frame profiles: passive only, filler only, both, and one (the largest edit mediator) with no natural class response. Wording: the neurons mediate part of the passive edit and have distinct frame profiles; no division of labour, no "the model's contrast runs through these neurons".

- **Main, the edit's path (B1, descriptive; one grouped bar in Figure 4, layer sweep to the appendix):** with every attention and MLP output of layers 8–23 clamped at the participle, 0.58 [0.51, 0.66] of D remains (the edited state carried by the residual stream). Single components (clamps interact, no additive slices): MLP14 +0.17 (first of 32), attention 23 +0.17, MLP15 +0.12, MLP11 +0.10; opposing: MLP23 −0.50, MLP12 −0.15, MLP20 −0.09. MLP 11 + 14 jointly 0.26 (reproduces phase 1).
- **Main, the natural link (B2, declared):** swapping only the v2 coordinate between the paired natural sentences removes 0.14 [0.09, 0.27] of the gap (CS1; the converse direction 0.46), and holding P fixed blocks 0.27 [0.18, 0.34] of that, beyond edit-matched controls (CS2). In natural units the chain through P is small (≈ 0.04 of G); say so.
- **Main, limit sentence (declared, NM1 / NM2 fail):** "the edit is read through these neurons; the model's own contrast is not detectably carried by them under this swap." m_nat(P) 0.06 [−0.02, 0.13] against natural-matched controls; all of MLPs 11 + 14 0.03, so the null is not specific to P. NM-class holds (+0.15 of G) but carries no class-specific claim without NM1. Exploratory: the top 50 / 100 neurons carry 0.09 / 0.11 of the natural gap.
- **Main, core neurons (top 5 by half-A direct effect on the *by* switch; same five as v's set):** labels from Pile contexts (R5-B7; n4241 blind, κ 0.91, `reports/pythia/v2_mech/b3a_annotation.md`): MLP11.n3462 reads an object missing after a verb; MLP14.n2114, n5407 and n4241 detect passive participles; MLP11.n4925 "other" (prepositions with a missing object, plus object gaps).
  - Causal shares of the edit (B3b, descriptive): together 0.167 of P's 0.188; n5407 0.093, n2114 0.048, n3462 0.009, n4241 0.008, n4925 −0.005; the three MLP14 neurons exceed every same-layer control (max 0.003); n3462 ranks 2 of 21 (the strongest MLP11 control 0.027), n4925 last.
  - Of the natural gap: only n2114 is detectable (0.058 [0.017, 0.119]); n5407, the largest edit mediator, carries none.
  - Frame profiles (natural T − I activation, B3c): n2114 passive only (0.30); n3462 and n4925 filler only (*what* 1.19 / 0.73, *that* 0.04 / −0.08, passive 0); n4241 both, at equal size (0.47 passive, 0.48 *what*); n5407 none in any frame.
- **Main, declared D1 holds:** the gap reader's class response is present with a filler and stronger than without one (fill − nofill +1.15 [1.06, 1.23]; also on the C8-gated lemmas). Say "stronger with a filler", not "only with a filler". Links §5 to the filler-gap paragraph of §3. Labelled a follow-up on reused activations.
- **Reading for the discussion:** the edit recruits one route (n5407) that natural sentences do not use, which fits the shuffled-label result (an edit along the margin axis drives a continuation pathway); the model's own contrast is carried more diffusely. Do not hide this; it is the honest limit of reading the mechanism through an edit.
